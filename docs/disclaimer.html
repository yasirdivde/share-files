Privacy

Files transferred through ShareFiles are sent directly from one device to another over an WebRTC data channel. No file, chunk, or file metadata ever passes through, or is stored on, any server operated by the developer. There is no backend, no database, and no logging of user activity. When you close the tab, nothing remains anywhere but on your own device and that of the recipient.

To establish the initial connection between two devices, the app uses PeerJS's public signaling service and public STUN servers (provided by Google) as part of the standard WebRTC handshake. Only ephemeral connection metadata (session IDs and network routing information) is exchanged through these services — never file contents. Those requests are subject to the respective providers' own privacy policies.

The site loads no third-party CDNs at runtime. All libraries, fonts, and styles are self-hosted.

A note on reliability

Because ShareFiles relies on a direct peer-to-peer connection, transfers can be affected by network conditions on either device. Very restrictive networks (such as some corporate or campus firewalls) may block the connection entirely. Transfers are also capped at a per-session size limit for memory safety. The developer is not responsible for interrupted, failed, or incomplete transfers caused by network conditions, device limitations, or browser behavior.
